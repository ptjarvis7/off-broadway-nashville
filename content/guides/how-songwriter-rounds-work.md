Nashville's best-known songwriter rounds sell seats on a schedule, so you have to be ready when tickets go on sale. At [The Bluebird Cafe](/venues/the-bluebird-cafe/), seats for a Saturday show release at 8am that Monday, and the popular ones can sell out in minutes. Once you're inside, the round runs itself. Three or four writers share the stage for about 90 minutes, taking turns on songs they wrote. What trips up first-timers happens before the first song, from booking windows to the $15 food and drink minimum.

## How a round plays out

Each writer plays three or four songs, one at a time, taking turns down the line in a fixed order. Many songs get a short setup first: where the idea came from, or which artist ended up recording it. Co-writing is the norm in Nashville, so you'll hear other writers' names dropped all night. Keep your phone's notes app handy if you want to look them up later.

Where the writers sit depends on the room. The Bluebird runs two setups. In the Round puts the writers in the middle of the room among the tables, facing each other. In the Row puts them up on the stage. The show listing tells you which one you're booking, so pick In the Round if sitting close to the writers matters most to you.

The Bluebird asks guests to be quiet during performances and keep conversations to between sets. Applause, laughter, even singing along are all encouraged. Phone photos are fine there, but video and audio recording aren't allowed. [The Listening Room Cafe](/venues/the-listening-room-cafe/) asks guests to keep talk to a whisper, and a loud table can get moved out to the front bar.

One of the first rounds I saw in Nashville was an evening showcase at [Live Oak](/venues/live-oak/). The first few writers were solid. Then Tanner Usrey, a singer-songwriter from Texas, took his first turn, and I knew right away he was different. Jason Michael Carroll went right after him, and when he played "Alyssa Lies," a 2000s hit I'd half forgotten, it felt like a trip back in time. That was the night I learned touring artists play these rounds too.

## Which kind of round to book

There are a few different types of rounds, and the show listing is where you'll tell them apart. For songs you'll recognize, book a hit-writer round. Most shows at the Listening Room Cafe and the Bluebird run this way. Listening Room Cafe listings name the artists who recorded each writer's songs, so you'll know what you're in for. If you only have one night in town, start here.

Some rounds build the whole setlist around a theme. The Listening Room Cafe has booked pop nights with writers behind songs for Kelly Clarkson and Kesha, so a round in this town doesn't have to mean country.

For a look at who's coming up next, try a showcase. At Live Oak on Demonbreun, the Grindhouse Nashville round seats veteran songwriters next to rising artists. Expect mostly new material, with the occasional radio hit mixed in.

For a night that takes no planning, go to a working round. [The Commodore Grille](/venues/commodore-grill/) in West End hosts a songwriter round with an open mic every night of the week. Music starts at 6pm Sunday through Thursday and 7pm on weekends. On Mondays, [The Green Light Bar](/venues/the-green-light-bar/) on 8th Avenue South tapes Playlisted, a live podcast where artists play one original song for a guest judge's feedback.

Unless you've already landed Bluebird tickets, I'd send a first-timer to the Listening Room Cafe. The room is intimate without feeling overly polished, there isn't a bad seat in the house, and the food is better than you'd expect. It feels like you're peeling back the curtain and getting a peek at the industry.

## Getting a seat

At the two best-known rooms, getting in comes down to timing. The Bluebird sells reservations online only, up to six seats per order. Tuesday through Thursday shows go on sale at 8am Central exactly one week ahead, so a Wednesday show opens the Wednesday before. Weekend shows all release on the Monday of that week at 8am. Seats sell by the chair, so a party of two may share a table with strangers. The name on the booking has to match your ID.

When family came to town wanting a Saturday show, I logged on 10 to 15 minutes before the 8am release. As soon as the clock hit 8am, I tried adding tickets to my cart and nothing happened. I refreshed the page, and the show had sold out within a minute. Saturdays are some of the hardest tickets to get, so if your trip allows it, aim for a weeknight show instead.

If you miss the release, the walk-up line is an option. Most Tuesday through Sunday shows hold 10 to 12 seats for people waiting at the door. Reserved seats still unclaimed once the show starts go to folks in line, too. Nobody can hold a spot for a friend. The Monday 6pm open mic is first come, first served, with no minimum.

At the Listening Room Cafe, the ticket doubles as your dinner reservation, but the seat itself isn't assigned. Doors open 30 minutes before showtime, and tables fill in the order people arrive. Show up when the doors open, especially on weekends. Arrive more than 15 minutes late and your seat may go to someone else.

Both rooms add a $15 food and drink minimum per person on top of the ticket. The Bluebird can charge it to no-shows, too. Grindhouse at Live Oak is free at the door, and the only cost is what you order.

## Can you see a songwriter round during the day?

Yes, and a lunch round is the easiest way to fit one around an evening already booked for the Opry or the Ryman. [3rd & Lindsley](/venues/3rd-and-lindsley/) in SoBro runs Backstage Nashville, a daytime hit-songwriter show, on Saturdays at 12:30pm. On Mondays at 12:30pm, the same room hosts Bluebird on 3rd, the Bluebird's own lunchtime round. That puts a Bluebird lineup a short ride from Broadway instead of out in Green Hills.

Daytime rounds tend to be more casual than night shows. Outside of ticketed events like these two, a lot of afternoon rounds are show-up-and-listen, with no ticket needed.

The Listening Room Cafe's front cafe also hosts free happy hour sets, an easy way to catch some music before dinner. Spots can be reserved online.
